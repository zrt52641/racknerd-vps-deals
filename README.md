## Choosing a US city (it's free to be picky here)

Pick the city nearest your users, because RackNerd prices all of its locations identically. Plenty of budget hosts either charge a location premium or limit you to one datacenter city; here the choice costs nothing, so it's worth ten seconds of thought.

Published cross-host latency figures put real numbers on the geography. Measured from a New York VPS, Chicago answers in about 18 ms, Miami around 25 ms, Dallas around 38 ms, and Seattle around 70 ms. Dallas-based providers advertise sub-20 ms response to roughly 90% of US internet users, since the city sits near the country's center inside one of its largest datacenter markets.

Some quick rules:

- **West Coast audience, or routes toward Asia:** Los Angeles or San Jose. LA is where the budget market concentrates — 26 of the 62 hosts in that CloudsPress roundup put servers there — so peering is dense.
- **East Coast or Europe-facing traffic:** New York or Atlanta.
- **National audience with no single center of gravity:** Dallas or Chicago.
- **Latency-critical projects like game servers:** match the game's own server region.
- **Amsterdam** sits on the list at the same price if most of your audience is European, though at that point you're no longer really buying a US VPS.

One practical habit: if a specific network route matters to your project, check the looking glass for that location before ordering instead of discovering a bad path after deploy.

## The fine print: what $11 a year doesn't include

Windows, managed backups, and someone else doing the sysadmin work. All three deserve spelling out.

- **Linux only.** These KVM plans come without a Windows Server option. If you need Windows for RDP workflows, ASP.NET, or MetaTrader EAs, Windows-focused budget hosts fill that gap — DatabaseMart advertises an Express Windows plan at $4.05/month on promo (regularly $8.99) covering Windows Server 2016 through 2025.
- **No managed backups.** Budget VPS means you own your backups: snapshots, restic, rsync to another box. Set something up before you need it, not after.
- **No cPanel license.** Not surprising when a license costs more per month than this server costs per year. Free panels like CyberPanel or Hestia are the standard route at this budget.
- **Fixed sizes on shared 1 Gbps uplinks.** Don't expect the elastic scaling of a big cloud; these are fixed-size plans. The transfer allowances (750 GB to 5 TB monthly) cover a lot, but a busy media site will walk through them.
- **Support is ticket-based.** RackNerd has been a fixture of the LowEndTalk budget scene for years and is one of the names people there routinely point to when someone asks for a cheap annual VPS. That track record is reassuring, and it still isn't the same as having an engineer tune your database for you.

One last pre-payment tip: read the refund terms on the order page. Yearly budget plans are not SaaS subscriptions with a cancel-anytime button, and the terms are the authoritative answer, not a review blog. This one included.
